{{PROJECT}}: how the agents work together

The owner talks to the coordinator. The product lead decides what to build (with a pre-groomer per heavy task), the architect decides how it is built, a fresh plan reviewer checks the plan against the owner's words, the coordinator owns the order, lanes build it, reviewers judge it, the coordinator merges it (an integrator only where that stops), the librarian keeps the docs short. Sources: docs/workflow/ (coordinator, product, architect, plan-review, librarian, pregroomer, telemetry, briefs), docs/PLANNING.md, docs/HANDOVER.md.

1. Who starts whom

The owner starts two sessions: the coordinator (their entry point) and, to plan, the product lead. The product lead starts its pre-groomers, the architect's gate and a fresh plan reviewer for each pass; everything else is started by the coordinator. Helpers (a cheap read) and the knowledge map can be used by any session.

3. T-shaped grooming

The product lead is the bar: it sees every spec, decision and the order. Each pre-groomer is a stem: it digs into one task with a fresh, small context and writes a report; the product lead checks it against the wide view, steers it, and writes the spec itself.

flowchart TB
  P["Product lead: all specs, decisions, order, architecture budgets"]
  P --- G1["Pre-groomer: task A
need, today, options, requirements"]
  P --- G2["Pre-groomer: task B"]
  P --- G3["Product lead grooms small task C itself"]
  G1 --> R1[".work/groom/A.md"] --> S1["spec A (product lead)"]
  G2 --> R2[".work/groom/B.md"] --> S2["spec B (product lead)"]
  G3 --> S3["spec C"]

4. The batch loop

Review before the slow tests. A green or green-with-notes review ends in the reviewer's own fixes and gate, so the executor retires at READY FOR REVIEW; only a real major goes back to it, and a fresh reviewer then checks the delta only (docs or tools only: on the mid model). A reviewer averages ~44k context per call, an executor 86-148k. A design question goes to the architect, a product question to the product lead.

  A["spec ready
How to build if heavy"] --> B["executor builds
types + unit, docs, report"]
  B -->|"READY FOR REVIEW
(executor retires)"| R{reviewer}
  R -->|"green, or only minors and nits"| F["reviewer fixes its own notes
in the executor's worktree, runs the gate"]
  F -->|"READY TO MERGE"| I["coordinator: integrate.py, gate, ship
(an integrator on a stop)"]
  R -->|"real blocker or major"| E2["executor fixes
(it knows the design)"]
  E2 -->|"READY FOR REVIEW"| R2{"fresh reviewer
delta only"}
  R2 --> F
  R -->|design question| T[Architect]
  T --> E2
  I --> APP[(running app)]

5. Clean stages

Every ~10 feature batches, on a 🔴 smell, or when known reds keep growing, the coordinator proposes a stage: no new features until the code and docs are clean again. The first one is heavy.

flowchart LR
  S[features paused] --> W[architect health sweep
refactor rows 🔴🟠🟡]
  W --> O[the coordinator places them
the owner sees the list]
  O --> X[lanes refactor
no behaviour change]
  X --> D[librarian pass
contradictions, compaction, budgets]
  D --> Z[features again]

6. The machinery around the agents

The scripts between the sessions. Every one saves model calls or context: one script call instead of many reads, a merge with no model at all. Opt-in parts are off until turned on: the pacer, prompt-polish and ctxpick live in your OpenCode config (deploy.py --global of the vibecode skill), autodeploy is "autodeploy": true in .work/ci/machine.json (tools/ci/README.md). The pacer is a gate in front of the coordinator: whatever would wake it waits one period and goes in as one call.

Doc budgets (what each session pays at its start): AGENTS.md 10 KB, docs/HANDOVER.md, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md and docs/PLANNING.md 30 KB each (docs/workflow/librarian.md). Each role's start reads total at most 60 KB, held by tools/work/check-docs.py. The product lead and the architect retire at ~140k context, before auto-compaction (~150k) cuts their wide view; the next one starts from the docs.

8. What relates, and how roles use their tools

tools/work/kmap.py builds a map of the project from what is already written (specs and their Links: lines, the backlog, decisions, the architecture's tables, docs/kmap/modules.md, imports, tests, git merges) with no model, in about a second, and answers "what relates to X" ranked and cut to a budget. tools/work/role-telemetry.py counts, per role, the raw commands a tool covers and the reads the map never gave; a flag climbs docs/workflow/telemetry.md's rungs, measured, not blocked.
